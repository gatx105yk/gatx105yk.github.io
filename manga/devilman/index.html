Devilmanデビルマン

Go Nagai (永井豪) · serialised 1972–1973 in Weekly Shōnen Magazine · completed

Devilman has been reprinted for fifty years by seven different publishers — five, if you count Kodansha's and Shogakukan's subsidiaries with their parents — and the ISBNs alone will not tell you which book you are looking at. This page lists every print edition recorded by Japan's National Diet Library, and — checked against Japanese retail on 2026-08-16 — what each one actually costs and whether a copy exists to buy at all.

Affiliate disclosure. The “buy” links on this page go to Rakuten and earn this site a commission if you order through them. That costs you nothing extra, and it does not decide what appears here: every edition on this page comes from the National Diet Library catalogue, including the ones nobody sells and the ones we earn nothing from.

What the check actually found

Exactly one edition on this page carries English text, and it is not complete:

  • Bilingual Edition バイリンガル版 (講談社インターナショナル, 2002–03) — 3 of 5 volumes obtainable, from ¥2,586

It is out of print, and it was published in Japan — which is the only reason it appears in a Japanese catalogue at all. English editions released by Western publishers never enter this data set, because they hold non-Japanese ISBNs and are not deposited with Japan's National Diet Library. So the honest statement is narrower than it looks: the Japanese market cannot sell you a complete English Devilman. Whether one exists elsewhere is a separate question this page does not yet answer.

One more thing worth knowing before you search: Devilman: Go Nagai presents (d/visual, 2004–05) has an English title on an English-looking cover, and it is listed on this page — but the text inside is Italian, not English. The National Diet Library records the text language as Italian for all five volumes. It is easy to buy by mistake.

Getting them out of Japan

Every price on this page is a Japanese domestic price

Rakuten has temporarily stopped accepting orders placed from seven countries — the United States, the United Kingdom, Australia, Singapore, New Zealand, Norway and France — over customs and tax handling. That list is Rakuten's own, published in its help centre under the heading 「海外からの注文受付を停止している対象国」, and it is exactly seven names long, not “including”. Being on it does not put these books out of reach; it changes the shape of the order.

Step 1

Get a Japanese address

Rakuten's own answer to the suspension is Rakuten Global Express, its forwarding service, which issues you a Japanese address and consolidates several orders into one parcel. Note how carefully Rakuten words the offer: delivery to a suspended country may be possible. Its country guides are where you find out, and for two of the seven the answer turns out to be no.

Step 2

Order to that address

Ordering to a Japanese address puts shops that will not ship abroad — which is most used-book sellers — back on the table, and it is the only route to the out-of-print editions above. It is not quite an ordinary domestic purchase, though. Rakuten Books does not say foreign cards are refused; it says there are restrictions on cards issued outside Japan, and that on some items and some orders they cannot be used — so your card may work, and you will find out at checkout. If it does not, the forwarder's proxy payment service will pay cash-on-delivery for you, but only at Rakuten Books and Rakuten 24, only if you applied and completed identity checks in advance, and it charges 10% of the order including domestic shipping, minimum ¥500, capped at ¥30,000 per order.

Step 3

Ship the consolidated parcel

Forwarding adds a handling fee plus international postage, so it pays to buy a whole set at once rather than one volume at a time. A ¥110 bunko volume is cheap; shipping it alone is not.

That is the short version. Buying manga from Japan sets out the whole route: which seven countries Rakuten stopped taking orders from, what the forwarder charges, what your card may and may not do, and what we could not measure.

If you are in the United States, only the expensive route is open. Rakuten Global Express will carry a parcel to America by Standard Express or by sea only when the goods inside it come to more than ¥410,000 including tax — around US$2,500, a floor Rakuten says it set with the American tariff regime in mind. Every edition on this page bought together does not come close to that, and Economy Air is not offered to America at all. What is left is Super Express, the DHL and FedEx service: Rakuten does offer it to America, with no minimum attached, and nothing on the list of goods it refuses for America is a printed book. It is the fastest of the four services, and Rakuten publishes no fare table for it — the price comes only from its own estimate tool, which we have not run. Measured 2026-08-04.

If you are in Norway, the route does not exist. Rakuten Global Express publishes a list of the countries it delivers to; on 2026-08-04 that list held 78 entries and Norway was not among them, though Sweden, Denmark, Finland and Iceland all were. There is no country guide for Norway to read either. For the remaining five — Britain, Australia, Singapore, New Zealand and France — the forwarder does deliver.

What this means for the prices above

The figures on this page are the shop price in Japan and nothing else. On top of them come the forwarder's fee, international postage, and import tax — and on this route the tax is charged on the postage and the handling fees as well as on the books. The rate depends on where you are: Rakuten states 20% for Britain, 10% for Australia, 9% for Singapore and 15% for New Zealand, and for France it publishes no tax note at all, so this page prints none. Where the tax is collected depends on a line Rakuten draws — £135 for Britain, A$1,000 for Australia, S$400 for Singapore. Below it Rakuten takes the tax when you pay for shipping and nothing is owed at the door; above it the carrier bills you at import instead. New Zealand names the same kind of line at NZ$1,000 and then says it makes no difference: the 15% is collected up front either way. For a complete used set the journey can cost more than the books.

Where these numbers come from

Editions, volumes and ISBNs come from the National Diet Library of Japan's bibliographic records, retrieved 2026-07-28 (214 records matched). Where the library record is ambiguous — an imprint with no series title, a volume count that could be partial — this page says so instead of guessing.

Cover prices at publication and imprint names come from openBD, the free Japanese book-metadata API, and are quoted only for the editions where openBD actually returns one. The rest print no price at all rather than an estimate.

What this page deliberately does not print. Page counts and trim sizes. Earlier versions of this page showed both. On 2 August 2026 we asked openBD for all 57 ISBNs listed here: it holds a record for 30 of them, and of those 30 it returns a page count for zero and a physical measurement for zero. The National Diet Library, for its part, records manga of this vintage as “1冊 ; 18cm” — one volume, eighteen centimetres — with no page count and no format name at all. So there was no source behind any of those figures, and they have been removed rather than dressed up. If a number is missing from this page, that is the reason.
